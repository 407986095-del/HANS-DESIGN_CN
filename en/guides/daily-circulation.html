How to plan circulation at home: trace routines and find conflicts

Trace repeated arrival, cooking, bathing and resting routines on the plan, then check crossings, doors, furniture and storage before judging circulation.

IN SHORT

Map real daily routes, then check crossings, detours and interruptions from doors or furniture. Circulation depends on household and site; there is no universal layout template.

READING ORDER

  1. Choose recurring daily routes
  2. Overlay routes on doors and furniture
  3. Compare options against real routines
  4. A circulation checklist for discussion
  5. Boundary: circulation dimensions need project review

Choose recurring daily routes

Choose key routines such as putting away shoes or bags, moving from kitchen to dining, or reaching the bathroom from a bedroom. Note users, timing and overlap.

Not every possible movement is a primary route. Focus on frequent routines, simultaneous use and conflicts that would matter.

Overlay routes on doors and furniture

Mark each route, then overlay door swings, pulled-out chairs, cabinet and drawer access, and everyday furniture. A route that looks clear may still conflict when things open.

Record crossings, unwanted direct sightlines, repeated detours and places where temporary items collect.

Compare options against real routines

Trace every route in each option. Ask what a change solves and what it creates. Does a shorter kitchen route reduce dining or storage space?

If a conflict comes mainly from household habits, design cannot replace resident agreement. If structure, fire safety or services constrain a change, verify the allowed scope first.

A circulation checklist for discussion

Beside the plan, list route, user, time and conflict. Separate verified site constraints from unknowns.

  • Entry: where do belongings go on arrival?
  • Kitchen and dining: do preparation, serving, seating and cleanup interrupt one another?
  • Rest: does a night-time bathroom route pass through a disruptive area?

Boundary: circulation dimensions need project review

This is a route-analysis method, not a universal clear-width or compliance specification. Furniture, doors, structure and applicable requirements affect dimensions and need project review.

CLUES & EVIDENCE

Resident descriptions, photos and floor plans can point to questions; they do not by themselves prove site dimensions, structural conditions, regulatory compliance or product performance. Verify conclusions against the site, applicable documents or professional advice, and record what remains unknown.
